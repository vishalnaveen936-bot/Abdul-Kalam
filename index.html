About A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.

He was an Indian aerospace scientist and served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007.

He worked with ISRO and DRDO and played an important role in India's space and missile programmes.

Major Achievements

Explore Dr. Kalam's contributions to India's space, defence and scientific programmes.

SLV-III Programme 1980

As Project Director, he led India's SLV-III programme. Its successful launch placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit.

ISRO Launch Vehicle Programme 1960s-1980s

While working at ISRO, he contributed to India's development of indigenous launch vehicle technology.

Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme 1980s-1990s

He led the missile development programme at DRDO and contributed to systems including Agni and Prithvi.

Technology Vision 2020 1990s

He helped shape a national technology vision for using science and technology to support India's development.

Scientific Adviser 1992-1999

He served as Scientific Adviser to the Defence Minister and Secretary of the Department of Defence Research and Development.

President of India 2002-2007

As India's 11th President, he became widely known for his close connection with students and young people.

Major Awards

Dr. Kalam received India's three major civilian honours for his contributions to the nation.

Padma Bhushan 1981

One of India's major civilian honours, awarded to Kalam for his contribution to science and engineering.

Padma Vibhushan 1990

This honour recognises distinguished service. It acknowledged his contributions to India's scientific and technological programmes.

Bharat Ratna 1997

India's highest civilian award, recognising his contributions to science, technology and public service.

Honorary Doctorates Various years

He received honorary doctorates from numerous universities and institutions in India and abroad.
